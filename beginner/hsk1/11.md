## 语法：吃饭 | Yǔ fǎ : chī fàn | Grammar: 吃饭 and the three meals

> **吃饭** (chī fàn), literally “eat rice”, means to have a meal. The meals are 早饭, 午饭 and 晚饭: “early”, “noon” and “evening” + 饭.

> **你吃饭了吗？** “Have you eaten?” is a friendly greeting around mealtimes, much like “How are you?”

| Hanzi | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 你 吃饭 了 吗？ | Nǐ chī fàn le ma ? | Have you eaten? |
| 我 吃饭 了。 | Wǒ chī fàn le . | I've eaten. |

## 语法：没有 | Yǔ fǎ : méi yǒu | Grammar: 没有 + verb: didn't

> To say something didn't happen, put **没有** before the verb and leave out 了: 我没有吃早饭 “I didn't have breakfast”. (不 is for habits and the future: 我不吃肉 “I don't eat meat”.)

> A short answer is just 没有.

| Hanzi | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 我 没有 吃 早饭。 | Wǒ méi yǒu chī zǎo fàn . | I didn't have breakfast. |
| 你 吃 早饭 了 吗？ —— 没有。 | Nǐ chī zǎo fàn le ma ? Méi yǒu . | Did you have breakfast? — No. |

## 语法：爱 | Yǔ fǎ : ài | Grammar: 爱 + verb: love doing

> Before a verb, **爱** means you love doing it, or do it a lot: 小明爱吃肉 “Xiaoming loves meat”. 不爱 + verb is a gentle “doesn't like”.

| Hanzi | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 小明 很 爱 吃 肉。 | Xiǎo míng hěn ài chī ròu . | Xiaoming loves meat. |
| 他 不 爱 吃 菜。 | Tā bú ài chī cài . | He doesn't like vegetables. |
