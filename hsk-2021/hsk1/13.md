## 语法：块 | Yǔ fǎ : kuài | Grammar: Money: 元, 块 and 钱

> Chinese money is counted in **元** (yuán), the word you see written on prices. In speech people say **块** (kuài): 十块 means the same as 十元, “ten yuan”. **钱** “money” is often added: 十块钱.

> Ask a price with **多少钱？** “how much money?”

| Hanzi | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 这 个 多少 钱？ | Zhè gè duō shǎo qián ? | How much is this? |
| 十 块 钱。 | Shí kuài qián . | Ten yuan. |

## 语法：还是 | Yǔ fǎ : hái shi | Grammar: 还是: A or B?

> **还是** (hái shi) joins two choices in a question: 茶还是水？ “Tea or water?” The question needs no 吗.

| Hanzi | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 你 要 茶 还是 牛奶？ | Nǐ yào chá hái shi niú nǎi ? | Would you like tea or milk? |

## 语法：的 | Yǔ fǎ : de | Grammar: Noun + 的: “a … one”

> A noun + **的** can stand for “one made of / belonging to …”: 肉的 “a meat one”, 我的 “mine”.

| Hanzi | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 这 个 包子 是 肉 的。 | Zhè gè bāo zi shì ròu de . | This is a meat bun. |
| 这 是 你 的 还是 我 的？ | Zhè shì nǐ de hái shi wǒ de ? | Is this yours or mine? |

## 对话一 | Duì huà yī | Dialog 1 · Buying bread

| Hanzi | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| Ming：这 个 面包 多少 钱？ | Zhè gè miàn bāo duō shǎo qián ? | How much is this bread? |
| Shop：五 块。 | Wǔ kuài . | Five yuan. |
| Ming：包子 呢？ | Bāo zi ne ? | And the buns? |
| Shop：包子 两 块 一 个。 | Bāo zi liǎng kuài yí gè . | The buns are two yuan each. |
| Ming：我 要 一 个 面包， 两 个 包子。 | Wǒ yào yí gè miàn bāo , liǎng gè bāo zi . | I'll have one loaf and two buns. |
| Shop：好， 九 块 钱。 | Hǎo , jiǔ kuài qián . | OK, nine yuan. |
| Ming：谢谢！ | Xiè xie ! | Thanks! |
