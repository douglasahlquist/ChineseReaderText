## 语法：几点 | Yǔ fǎ : jǐ diǎn | Grammar: Telling the time with 点

> Say the hour with a number + **点** (diǎn): 三点 “three o'clock”, 十二点 “twelve o'clock”. Two o'clock is 两点, not 二点. Ask with 几点？

| Hanzi | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 现在 几 点？ | Xiàn zài jǐ diǎn ? | What time is it? |
| 现在 两 点。 | Xiàn zài liǎng diǎn . | It's two o'clock. |

## 语法：上午下午 | Yǔ fǎ : shàng wǔ xià wǔ | Grammar: Parts of the day come first

> The part of the day comes before the hour, just as in dates the bigger unit comes first: **上午十点** “10 a.m.”, **晚上八点** “8 p.m.”. And the whole time phrase goes before the verb: 我们十一点去教会.

| Hanzi | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 上午 十 点。 | Shàng wǔ shí diǎn . | Ten in the morning. |
| 我们 十一 点 去 教会。 | Wǒ men shí yī diǎn qù jiào huì . | We go to church at eleven. |

## 语法：什么时候 | Yǔ fǎ : shén me shí hou | Grammar: When? 什么时候

> **什么时候** (shén me shí hou) “what time / when” goes where the time would go: 你什么时候来？ → 我十点来。

| Hanzi | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 你 什么 时候 来？ | Nǐ shén me shí hou lái ? | When are you coming? |

## 语法：了 | Yǔ fǎ : le | Grammar: 了 for something that has happened or changed

> At the end of a sentence, **了** (le) shows that something has happened or that the situation is now different: 他来了 “he's here (he has come)”, 十点了 “it's ten o'clock now”. There is much more to 了 later; for now, notice it marks something new.

| Hanzi | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 安娜 来 了！ | Ān nà lái le ! | Anna's here! |
| 十 点 了。 | Shí diǎn le . | It's ten o'clock now. |
