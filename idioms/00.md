# 成语 | Chéng yǔ | About the idioms

> **成语** (chéng yǔ) are set phrases, most of them four characters long, that come from old stories, poems and sayings. They pack a lot of meaning into a few words, and educated speakers use them all the time.

> These pages hold the **6,323 most common idioms** that have an English meaning, ranked from the most used to the least and split into groups of up to 1,000. Each card shows the idiom, its pinyin, a Chinese explanation and the English. Tap an idiom to hear it.

> The full collection has **30,895 idioms**. The other 24,572 have pinyin and a Chinese explanation but no English yet. They are in the “Chinese Idioms 成语” spreadsheet, and may be added here later.

> Sources: the idioms and Chinese explanations are from the open Xinhua dictionary data (github.com/pwxcoo/chinese-xinhua, MIT licence); the English is from CC-CEDICT (CC BY-SA 4.0); the ranking is from the wordfreq word frequencies.
